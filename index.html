What this experiment can establish

Two discrete distributions

f1(n) = n−β / ζ(β)
f2(n) = n1−β − (n + 1)1−β

For both, n ≥ 1 and 1 < β ≤ 4. At β = 2, f1 assigns about 60.8% probability to a frequency of one; f2 assigns 50%. The difference on common small counts matters even when the tails have a similar exponent.

Checks before the corpus run

All 39,600 outer simulated cases completed. The 18 primary cells each used 2,000 outer samples and 100 refitted inner draws; their 0.05 rejection rates ranged from 4.2% to 5.4%. All predeclared primary calibration and six large-sample estimator-recovery screens passed, with no recorded numerical exception. An independent reviewer recomputed the gates from the raw records.

The simulated null treats word types as iid draws. Calibration under that null does not establish independence among real words. Supplementary extreme-size cells are diagnostics, not universal calibration guarantees.

Calibration cells, including extreme-size diagnostics

Primary cells have 2,000 outer tests; supplementary cells have 200. The rejection intervals use confidence 1 − 0.01/18. At the 0.05 cutoff the reference rank-grid fraction is 5/101 ≈ 4.95%; the primary screening band is that value ±3 percentage points. This is a bounded screen, not proof of exact uniformity. Mean estimator error can be appreciable at small N; only N = 30,000 is subject to the predeclared ±0.005 recovery screen.

Scope, provenance and reproducibility

This is an independent implementation, reviewed first by fresh-context AI agents of the same model family and then by Gemini 4 Carbon of a different model family. These reviews provide context, production-role and model-family independence; they are not independent human replication. No code was copied from another paper-claims project. Previous projects served as high-level precedent only.

The September proposal already contained pilot results. The frozen amendment replaced its ambiguous uniformity and parameter-recovery criteria with explicit calibration screens and a bounded precision audit. Pass A, pass B and the precision follow-up use fixed seed prefixes 2026111703, 2026111704 and 2026111705, with separate model and PG identifiers. No best-seed selection is used.

The complete archive contains 31,075 vectors and 1,961,738,645 tokens. No book is removed. All five length anchors agree with the source checks; the two scoped Figure 4 exponent anchors round to 1.96 and 1.89. The two historical maximum-book identities depend on unreleased author seeds, so matching exponents is not a replication of that selection.

Full f3 reproduction, the corpus length-tail fit, genre explanations, alternate tokenizations, SPGC overlap and holdout testing are outside this experiment. Frequency vectors alone do not establish a cause for the length pattern. No novelty claim is made.
