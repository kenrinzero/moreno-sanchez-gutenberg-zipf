# Amendment 1 — proposed execution contract

2026-11-17. Pending fresh-context review and explicit freeze. The original draft and exposed September pilots remain preserved. At authorship, only archive custody and deterministic vector/length checks have run; no estimated exponent, KS bootstrap or corpus acceptance output has been inspected.

## Models and arithmetic

Use f1 and f2, a=1, constrained 1<beta<=4, full infinite support and type count N. Stable compressed likelihoods weight each distinct frequency by its number of types. MLE algorithms must return an explicit upper-bound solution when appropriate. A numerical lower guard is beta=1+1e-8; crossing that guard is a recorded numerical exception, not an interior estimate. Use an absolute beta root/bracket tolerance at most1e-9; any score is normalized per observation and cannot substitute for a parameter-error bound without accounting for curvature. Cross-check estimates against an independent high-precision likelihood implementation to 2e-7 on nondegenerate test cases. Zero observations, nonpositive frequencies and invalid multiplicities are errors.

Discrete KS uses empirical/theoretical survival at each observed u and u+1; no continuous jump formula. Primary simulation exceedance counts D_sim>=D_observed; retain the count of exact floating-point ties and the strict-exceedance count for sensitivity. A comparison tolerance is not added to the threshold. Each null sample has N type counts and its beta is refitted under the same constraints.

LR uses the population variance of log f1-log f2, primary p_LR<0.05, and explicit sign. Also report the sample-variance version. If variance is zero and R=0, p_LR=1; if variance is zero and R is nonzero, flag a degenerate asymptotic calculation and exclude from significance categories rather than assert p=0. Report such exceptions as their own category; never discard their books.

## Sampling and numerical tails

Use exact category decomposition: multinomial counts for frequencies 1,...,64 and a remaining tail category. Sample that many frequencies conditionally on n>=65. The f2 tail uses floor(65*U^(-1/(beta-1))); f1 uses rejection from that f2 tail with the envelope attained at 65. This is algebraically the same iid model as sampling every type separately; validate against a separate direct method. Probabilities must be finite, nonnegative and sum to one within 2e-12; retain the analytically computed survival tail for the check, not an unexplained renormalization.

Use a high-precision fallback for inverse draws whose integer exceeds 2^52, and for fast-path values within 64*machine_epsilon*max(x,1)*(1+abs(log(x))) of an integer. Use exact binary U and beta inputs; increase decimal precision until two successive floors agree with a residual error bound smaller than their distance to the adjacent integers. Exact integer-power cases may use rational arithmetic and integer roots. No ambiguous floor is silently accepted. For beta<=2, do not validate the sampler by convergence of its mean count or token length: the unbounded model has infinite first moment. Validate masses, survival and log-statistics instead. Store exceptional support as Python arbitrary integers. Likelihood and survival calculations may use log(n) with certified asymptotic expansions at large n; check them against mpmath at 80 or more digits. Record exceptional-draw counts. High-precision oracle tolerances: logpmf absolute error<=1e-10 per observation, survival and final KS absolute error<=1e-12. Near a bootstrap comparison where accumulated numerical error, including fitted-beta uncertainty, could change its sign, recompute both fitted betas and KS values at higher precision rather than resolving the comparison with a widened tie tolerance. No int64 wrap, clipping, upper truncation, replacement draws or silent exception retries. Failed numerical cases remain visible and stop the affected gate.

## Calibration

1. Deterministic mathematical tests: source-table sums; support normalization/tail identities; MLE agreement with independent high-precision optimization; direct-grid discrete KS on small samples with large gaps; sampler inversion boundary cases; LR symmetry and degeneracy; upper-bound fits; compressed versus expanded likelihood. Tests at beta={1.5,2,3,4}, plus beta=1.1 for stress, are not all assertions of unbiased recovery.
2. Primary nested calibration: each model at true beta={1.5,2,3}, N={300,3000,30000}, with 2,000 outer samples per cell and 100 refitted inner simulations. Total 36,000 outer tests. Each cell reports rejection rates at p0={0.01,0.05,0.20,0.50}, ties, fitted-beta bias/SD and all numerical exceptions. The 101-point uniform rank law is a diagnostic benchmark only: composite-null bootstrap statistics are not exactly exchangeable. The inclusive ideal rejection fraction at .05 is 5/101, not .05.
3. Calibration screening gate: for each of the 18 cells, the two-sided Clopper-Pearson interval for the rejection probability at p0=.05, at confidence 1-.01/18, must be entirely inside [5/101-.03, 5/101+.03]. This is an explicitly bounded absolute calibration-error screen, not proof of exact calibration or an independence model for real words. Any numerical exception fails this gate. Other thresholds and the full discrete grid are reported, without an invented continuous-uniform KS test.
4. Estimator recovery: numerical cross-implementation agreement is the hard correctness check. Report ensemble bias and Monte Carlo SE at every N. Do not require every beta estimate, nor even every small-N mean, to be within .005: legitimate O(1/N) bias can exceed that. At N=30000 and beta={1.5,2,3}, require the simultaneous 1-.01/6 normal Monte Carlo interval for each mean error to lie wholly inside [-.005,.005]; this is an ensemble recovery screen. Constrained beta=4 behavior is a separate boundary test and is not treated as unbiased.
5. Supplementary diagnostics: beta={1.5,2,3} at N={27,84,210347}, 200 outer samples per cell and100 refitted inner draws. These bracket the observed corpus type counts and remain reported diagnostics rather than broad claims of uniform calibration. Boundary/all-one deterministic cases are separate.
6. Benchmark on synthetic tasks before full calibration; the benchmark does not count as the calibration run and does not change calibration seeds or thresholds. Use at most eight CPU workers initially. Document projected duration. Numerical repairs require a pre-run amendment and rerunning affected calibration; data-result-driven tolerance changes are prohibited.

## Precision follow-up and claims

All books get two-pass 2x2 status tables, switch rates and Cohen kappa; a pooled B=200 estimate is diagnostic and never replaces the paper-like B=100 reproduction. The exposed seed's net +/-4% prediction means relative change in accepted count from A to B, not percentage points. Its 500-2,000 f2 switches is reported as an exposed prediction, not a calibration gate.

Bound the expensive follow-up before observing fits: for each model select up to50 books from each pass-A K_ge stratum {1..3,4..5,6..10,11..20}, ordered by SHA-256 of UTF-8 '2026111705:model:PGid'. No stratum refilling. These up to200 books/model receive a fresh fixed B=10,000 run; do not reuse the selecting trials and do not stop early by outcome. Report pointwise95% fixed-sample Clopper-Pearson intervals and theoretical worst-case MC SE .005. Classify a selected book/model as rejected when its interval upper endpoint<.05, non-rejected when its lower endpoint>=.05, and unresolved otherwise. These are pointwise conditional Monte Carlo intervals, not a simultaneous guarantee over up to400 selected book/model pairs and not natural-language model uncertainty. The remaining books are explicitly flagged as not reaching the high-precision target; this is a stratified subset audit, not a claim of per-book precision across the entire corpus. Report the selected list and stratum coverage. No genre or holdout inference follows.
