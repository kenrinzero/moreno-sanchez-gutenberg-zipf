### Two Cross-Family Forensic Additions on the Five Table Mismatches

1. **Deterministic corpus-wide Vuong LR parity (`2,178 vs 2,176` and `20,856 vs 20,857`):** Summing Table 2 across all four mutually exclusive KS rows (`f1 only + f2 only + both + neither`) removes KS Monte Carlo partitioning entirely. Across all 31,075 books, published Table 2 has `2,176` significant-$f_1$ and `20,857` significant-$f_2$ texts; this implementation yields `2,178` (+2 books, 0.0064%) and `20,856` (-1 book, 0.0032%), with three books sitting within $5 \times 10^{-4}$ of $|z_{\text{LR}}| = 1.96$ (`PG 12270` at $z = -1.95952$, `PG 1605` at $z = +1.95965$, `PG 26747` at $z = +1.95978$). Thus the `-54` miss in `table2 / Neither / Significant f1` (`456 vs 510`) is **not** an LR calculation divergence — it is the mechanical transfer (`-54 + 56 = +2`) of 56 significant-$f_1$ books from `Neither` into `f1 only` (`1,722 vs 1,666`) caused by higher KS acceptance. Furthermore, the maximum accepted lengths in Pass A (`PG 43016`, $L = 241,358$ for $f_1$; `PG 5140`, $L = 348,992$ for $f_2$) match the paper's Figure 9 upper endpoints to the exact token.
2. **Exact two-pass variance identity rules out pure 100-draw seed luck:** Conditional on the fixed 31,075 books, independent per-book Bernoulli acceptance indicators $A_i, B_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\pi_i)$ satisfy $\text{Var}_{\text{MC}}(\sum_i A_i) = \sum_i \pi_i(1-\pi_i) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\text{Switches}(A, B)]$. From the observed switches (`530` for $f_1$, `1,446` for $f_2$, `301` for `both`), the single-pass Monte Carlo SD of the corpus total under identical code is `16.28` books ($f_1$), `26.89` books ($f_2$), and `12.27` books (`both`). Pass A's excesses over published Table 1 (`+200` on $f_1$, `+181` on $f_2$, `+89` on `both`) correspond to $+12.3\sigma_{\text{MC}}$, $+6.7\sigma_{\text{MC}}$, and $+7.3\sigma_{\text{MC}}$. This mathematically confirms that pure Monte Carlo seed realization under identical code cannot explain the gap, while supporting the report's disciplined refusal to guess which undocumented author detail (e.g., direct likelihood-maximization stopping tolerance in Numerical Recipes `brent`, discrete-KS jump convention, or cutoff handling) produced the 2016 counts.
